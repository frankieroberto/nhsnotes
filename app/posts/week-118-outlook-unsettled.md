A Friday in the office. I was the only one there. Outlook wasn’t working due to the global outage. My calendar was remarkably free of meetings. I took the opportunity to scratch an itch and create a new prototype.

If you’ve been following these weeknotes, you’ll know that I’ve been floating the idea of letting users add appointments to the NHS App themselves. In some ways it’s a poor substitute for doing the internal work to have them appear automatically, foisting the admin burden onto users instead. But at this point, getting all NHS appointments into the app feels like it’s many, many years away. So allowing users to add their own missing appointments might be a useful pragmatic option.

It also means that users could add appointments for services beyond those covered by the NHS in England: say, private dental or optician appointments, or appointments with the NHS in Wales or Scotland, or even medical appointments whilst abroad on holiday.

The process of prototyping this was remarkably freeing. Rather than looking at what data the app currently has for appointments and working out how best to display it, I could flip my thinking to be user-centric: what information would users want to add about their appointments? And how does that differ when adding an upcoming appointment versus one in the past?

The same flip applies to the interface as a whole. Mike and I have discussed an idea: what if the main view of the app was a timeline? Instead of a series of menus for things like prescriptions, vaccinations and test results, would it make sense to show these in a combined chronological feed, perhaps with filters or search? I tried that out too.

I’m also interested in how enabling self-entered appointments might mean a different approach to research and testing. At the moment, two common approaches are to observe how users use the current live app with their real data in it, or to give them a prototype with mocked-up data, imagining a hypothetical scenario. Instead, we could ask them to use a prototype for a longer period of time, adding the real information they’d expect to see.

I ended the Friday with a prototype I was fairly happy with, and then did some more tweaking over the weekend. I’m now trying it out, adding all my previous medical history and any upcoming appointments, to see how it feels.

There are lots of rough edges, but I think it shows promise. I’m able to add items fairly quickly, and once they’re in, I get a glanceable view of my own care that the app doesn’t currently give me. I’m interested to see what others make of it.

Not bad for a Friday internet snow day.
