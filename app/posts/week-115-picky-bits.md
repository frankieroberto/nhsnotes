My attention this week continued to be scattered, picking up bits of work across a few different strands.

In brief:

* writing up previous work on the [getting a test](/posts/week-111-mind-the-gap/#getting-a-test) sketch
* exploring more options for enhancing HPV test result pages in the app 
* early planning for an event I’m proposing for next year
* working on the [prototype kit](https://prototype-kit.service-manual.nhs.uk), bringing some features of the app prototype kit into the main one so that the two can be merged
* adding styles for lists and forms to the iOS design system - these will likely be the foundation of the more detailed screens in the app
* making a first contribution to the Android design system - I started small, setting up the corner radiuses
* thinking about appointments in the app and how we might let users have more control over them, adding missing ones or adding notes - this idea has some skeptics but I’m keen to explore it
* taking part in a ‘deep dive’ into our team with the prevention services leadership team – a chance for us to show them what we’re all about

## Links

* [Why and how we used co-design to understand shisha smoking history](https://design-history.prevention-services.nhs.uk/lung-health-check/2026/10/why-and-how-we-used-codesign/) from the lung cancer screening team describes community workshops where people who smoke shisha mapped their own smoking timelines and drafted the questions they’d ask
* [Testing changes to the NTDD batching screen in BS Select](https://design-history.prevention-services.nhs.uk/breast-screening-select/2026/10/ntdd-batch-usability-findings/) from the BS Select team is a good reminder that removing a seemingly redundant checkbox or radio button can have knock-on effects on how people work
* [Redesigning the service landing page to improve conversion](https://design-history.prevention-services.nhs.uk/nhs-health-check-online/2026/10/redesigning-the-service-landing-page/) from the NHS Health Check online team explains how moving away from plain text and standard components to a more visual, story-led page increased click-through
* [Improving the medicines record experience in the NHS App](https://design-history.nhsapp.service.nhs.uk/prescriptions/2026/10/improving-the-medicines-record-experience-in-the-nhs-app/) from the NHS App prescriptions and medicines team runs through a set of small, low-risk changes, such as swapping ‘acute’ for ‘one-off’ and removing non-clickable white cards
