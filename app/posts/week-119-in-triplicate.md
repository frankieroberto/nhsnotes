A week of small steps on three fronts.

## NHS App design system updates

I’ve been helping to make some updates to the NHS App design system, both the existing web-based one and the new native iOS one.

For the web one, we released [version 5.4.0](https://github.com/nhsuk/nhsapp-frontend/releases/tag/v5.4.0). This adds a new variation of the [profile card](https://design-system.nhsapp.service.nhs.uk/components/card-links/#proxy-card-links) when you are in ‘proxy mode’, managing health services for someone else. We’ve also deprecated the [app-specific colour palette](https://design-system.nhsapp.service.nhs.uk/styles/colour/), as the pale and dark colour variations have now been upstreamed into the main [NHS colour palette](https://service-manual.nhs.uk/design-system/styles/colour).

For the iOS design system, we’ve released [version 2.2.0](https://design-system.nhsapp.service.nhs.uk/ios/version-history/#2.2.0---20-november-2026). This adds new styles for forms, lists and buttons, as well as a coloured header area. It remains a work in progress, as development of the native app transformation continues, but we’ve now got more teams using it and we’re getting clearer on which things we’re confident about, and which need more research.

## Linking appointments

I’ve done a little bit more tinkering on the [app prototype](/posts/week-118-outlook-unsettled/) I made last week, adding the ability to link appointments together, such as referrals or follow-ups. Being able to show this visually in the app helps, I think, to bring the view closer to how patients might remember and describe a chain of events. A call to 111 which led to a GP appointment which led to a hospital appointment which led to an operation. Getting the data from the different bits of the NHS to be able to actually do this automatically would no doubt be a huge challenge - but demonstrating it through self-entered data helps to show the possibility.

I’ve given a few colleagues access to the prototype to get their feedback. More tweaking ahead.

## Explaining HPV test results

Our team is still looking to [improve test results](/posts/week-109-near-and-far/) in the app. As one step towards this, we’ve been looking at how we might add some explanatory text to HPV test results to describe what it is, and what the result might mean. This is important because research shows that not all patients realise that the HPV test is part of cervical screening, which is understandable. The acronym, and even the longer human papillomavirus term, isn’t widely known.

My role in this is mainly as a facilitator, bringing together the multiple teams who can progress this. It sits at the boundary between the NHS.UK teams (who look after this kind of content), the NHS App teams, and the cervical screening teams. The hard bit is working out who writes and approves the content, who develops the code to add it to the app, and who takes it through all the clinical assurance steps.

We’re all keen to avoid triplicating the paperwork if we can avoid it, and to find ways to collaborate without stepping on toes.
